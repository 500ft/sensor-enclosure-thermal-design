## Physical Box, Materials, and Geometry

Not applicable. The content is the Pi theorem: a complete relation reduces to `psi(Pi_1, ..., Pi_i, r', r'', ...) = 0` with **`i = n - k`**, where `k` is the number of fundamental units required; and two systems are physically similar with respect to a relation only if **all** the `Pi`s and **all** the same-kind ratios `r` are unchanged.

### Pros

- Supplies the formal warrant for the "collapse" claim: with the other groups and ratios fixed, `Pi_1` is constant regardless of the unknown form of the relation.
- Gives the selection rule that the target quantity must be a factor of **only one** group — directly auditable against this project's choice of normalised bias as the output group.

### Cons

- **Non-uniqueness, stated by Buckingham himself (p. 350):** several different triads of fundamental units can be used, so other equivalent equations "present a different appearance". Only the count `i = n - k` is invariant; the members are not. This project's five groups are therefore **one of several equivalent sets**, not the set.
- **Completeness is a precondition** (p. 345): the reduction holds only if no relevant quantity has been overlooked.
- Dimensionless same-kind ratios are outside the theorem's reach: it cannot tell how they enter, so they must be carried as extra arguments, not dropped.

## Selection Lessons for This Project

- Cite for: the Pi theorem and the count `i = n - k`; the definition of physical similarity; **the non-uniqueness of the group set**; the completeness precondition; the rule that the target quantity appears in exactly one group.
- Do **not** cite for: any heat-transfer claim, any regime boundary, any explanation of why a collapse fails in one regime, or the modern rank-based statement of the theorem.
