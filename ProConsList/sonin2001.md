# Sonin (2001): The Physical Basis of Dimensional Analysis

**Source:** https://web.mit.edu/2.25/www/pdf/DA_unified.pdf  
**Evidence boundary:** Full text read 2026-09-22 (sections 3.1–3.3 and 4 verbatim). **Venue flag: departmental course monograph (MIT ME, 2nd ed., 57 pp.) — not peer-reviewed, no DOI, no ISBN, no publisher.** Cite alongside `buckingham1914`, never alone.

## Physical Box, Materials, and Geometry

Not applicable. Content: the four-step nondimensionalisation procedure; the theorem that the number of independent quantities reduces from `n` to `n - k`; and §4's fixed-value-quantity count `N = (n - k) - (n_F - k_F)`, which is the defensible accounting for why a DOE that holds some properties fixed ends with fewer groups.

### Pros

- Treats exactly the two failure modes this project is exposed to: an **incomplete** set of variables "may destroy the analysis", and **superfluous** variables "rob it of its power" by adding groups that do nothing.
- §3.3 gives the a-posteriori warrant this project actually relies on: support may be obtained by showing data correlate in the predicted dimensionless form, provided there are enough data to show that neither an important quantity was missed nor an irrelevant one included.
- Confirms independently of Buckingham that the result does not depend on which dimensionally independent subset is chosen — different-looking but algebraically equivalent relations.

### Cons

- Explicitly notes completeness "is not an absolute matter, but depends on how we choose to circumscribe the problem": asserting a variable does not matter is asserting a **regime**.
- Does not cover incomplete similarity / intermediate asymptotics, which is the sharpest available explanation for "collapses in one regime, fails in another".
- Gives only the number of groups, never the form of the relation.

## Selection Lessons for This Project

- Cite for: the modern Pi-theorem statement; independence from the choice of subset; the missing-variable and superfluous-variable cautions; the fixed-quantity group count; and the a-posteriori-collapse-as-evidence argument (with its "enough data" proviso).
- Do **not** cite for: anything thermal; and never present it as a journal or book of record — label it a course monograph.
