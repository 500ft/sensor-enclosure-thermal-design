# Literature review — model inputs, printed materials, propagation, method (2026-09-22)

**Purpose.** The repository's 32 prior sources gave it breadth on radiation shields, low-cost
air-quality calibration, deployment reliability and standards. They did **not** support the numbers
the model actually runs on. This review adds **27 sources** chosen against four specific gaps, and
its main product is the **model-input provenance table** in §2: every constant in
[`thermal_bias.ASSUMPTIONS`](../analysis/thermal_bias.py) mapped to a citation or to an explicit
"still uncited".

**Reading discipline.** 25 of 27 were read in full. Two are recorded as **full-read pending**
(`churchill1975`, `barcohen1984`) with bibliographic identity verified and **no content attributed**.
Where a source was read as a preprint, author manuscript or lab-report version rather than the
publisher's version of record, its ProConsList entry says so. Several are flagged non-archival:
`sonin2001` (MIT course monograph), `ostrach1953` (NACA report), `morgan2017` (unreviewed LANL
report), `ishizuka2012` (conference proceedings).

**Headline: this review found errors in the repository, not just gaps.** Three of the four physical
constants examined are unsupported or mis-referenced, and the handbook conductivity used for the
Biot argument is contradicted by three independent measurements.

**Cross-cutting (`berdahlbretz1997`, for exactly this lumped form):** a 10 % error in `(h_r + h_c)`
gives a 10 % error in `(T_surface − T_air)`, and "knowledge of `(h_r + h_c)` with an accuracy of ten
percent is not feasible because of the large uncertainty in the convection coefficient `h_c`." So
**constant 2 dominates the error budget**; tightening ε and α buys little until `h` is calibrated or
carried with an honest band. The same paper gives `h_r = 5.5 W/m²K at ε = 0.9`, which this project
may cite for its radiative linearization.

## 3. The Biot claim, corrected

**Keep the claim; rewrite the justification.** "Wall conduction is first-order and classical
metal-shield physics ignores it" is *better* supported by measured printed data than by the handbook
set — but not for the reason originally given.

- Drop **k = 0.13 for printed PLA**: contradicted by three independent measurements (0.182–0.267).
- Attribute the high-Bi end to **through-layer anisotropy** (`janek2026`: 0.1039 W/mK, ratio 1.60),
  **reduced infill** (`tychaniczkwiecien2025`: −30 % at 40 %; `lopes2023`: pattern alone moves
  effective k by 82 %), and **foaming filaments** (`baraboi2026`: PLA Aero 0.114).
- **State the direction of measurement.** Only `janek2026` resolves it, and the ratio is 1.60, not
  an order of magnitude.
- **Caveat none of these sources addresses:** a 3 mm wall printed with perimeter shells may be
  near-solid regardless of the infill setting, so the infill-derived k values are an upper bound on
  the effect for a real wall.

Measured envelope at 3 mm, holding the project's own implied `h ≈ 6.8–25 W/m²K`: **Bi ≈ 0.07–0.72**
(and 0.20–1.3 if the wall is genuinely infilled). Same order as claimed; the **upper bound is worse**
than claimed. At Bi > 1 the isothermal-wall assumption is not questionable, it is violated.

## 4. Self-heating: what the origin papers actually say

**Barkjohn's quoted "2.7 to 5.3 °C" is not a range.** It is two single numbers welded together:
5.3 °C / 24.3 % from `holder2020`, 2.7 °C / 9.7 pp from `malings2020`. Neither paper reports a
range, and **`barkjohn2021` contains no T/RH measurement of its own.**

They also assert **two different mechanisms**, which `barkjohn2021` silently merges: Holder says the
sensor is "likely … heated from the sensor electronics"; Malings says the plastic shell "can trap
heat inside the unit." Neither measured it.

**No source located in this review reports a measured internal dissipation.** `shlipak2025` is the closest and the most valuable:
it treats internal heat generation as a first-class modelled quantity, brackets low-cost monitors at
**1–5 W**, and runs a per-watt sensitivity — but states plainly that the wattages are **estimated
from rated electrical input as an upper bound**, and PurpleAir is not in its table.

**Corrected 2026-09-24.** This section previously concluded that the literature's only
evidence-based cause separation points at **solar, not electronics**. That inference is
**withdrawn**: near-zero night bias does not imply near-zero dissipation, because outward long-wave
loss can exactly offset electronics heating (verified in this repo's model: `Q = 0.102972 W` gives a
`-1.2e-8 °C` night bias). What the observations support is a constraint on the **combined heat
balance**, not attribution. The underlying observations are still worth recording:
`shlipak2025` measured internal temperature converging to ambient at night ("at night, simulated
temperatures, ambient temperatures, and observed temperatures are nearly identical") with the largest
deviation under variable solar; and `holder2020`'s only quasi-empirical attribution test concluded
"possible that the higher measured temperature is partly due to radiant heating from the sun." A continuous electronics-driven offset need not vanish at night, but neither does its absence
follow from a small net bias — the two effects are of opposite sign and can cancel.

**The opening, stated in bounded form (revised 2026-09-24):** *no source located in this
repository's search record reports a measured internal dissipation for a low-cost air-quality
enclosure together with an attribution of the observed T/RH bias to it.* This is a statement about
the search record, **not** a claim that no such work exists; it is provisional until a documented
broader search. There is also an unexploited
discriminating signature — PurpleAir's bias is a near-constant offset while the RAMP's is diurnal
(`holder2020`) — which nobody has followed up.

## 5. Propagation to calibration (SQ3)

- **PM:** `jayaratne2018` gives the transfer function — response flat below **~78 % RH**, then
  +80 % of reading by 89 %, hysteresis to ~50 %. Consequence for this project: because internal RH
  reads 10–24 points dry, a correction keyed to internal RH can place genuinely humid ambient air
  *below* the 78 % threshold. *(That consequence is this project's inference, not the paper's.)*
- **Gas:** `samad2020` publishes RH+T correction polynomials in ppb for four Alphasense B4 sensors,
  with a 10–25 °C usable window and NO₂ zero drift toward −200 ppb at high temperature.
  `popoola2016` gives the field-scale magnitude (~250 ppb NO baseline error) and the structural
  finding that temperature acts on the **baseline, not the gain**.

## 6. Method

- **Nondimensionalization:** `buckingham1914` (Pi theorem, `i = n − k`) and `sonin2001`. Both state
  the **group set is non-unique** — this project's five groups are one of several equivalent sets, a
  caution Study A did not previously carry. `sonin2001` also supplies the warrant *and* the warning
  for a collapse test: a failed collapse means a missing variable **or** a regime change.
- **Natural convection:** `ostrach1953` supplies the precedent that a closed-form law is
  regime-limited ("Grashof number alone determines the type of flow"), but it is an external flat
  plate — **not** an enclosure. The archival enclosure/channel sources remain **full-read pending**.
- **Uncertainty:** `jcgm2008gum`. Its §5.2.2 worked example *is* this pilot's design — one reference
  serving several units. Because `r = +1`, uncertainties add **linearly**, not in quadrature;
  ignoring that understated the answer by ~3× in the Guide's own example, i.e. it errs
  **anti-conservatively**. §5.2.4 gives the fix: re-parameterize so the shared reference enters once
  as an explicit independent input. And `U95 ≤ 0.5 °C` means `u_c ≤ 0.25 °C`, with the four G.6.6
  conditions demonstrated rather than assumed.
- **Held-out validation:** `arlot2010` supplies the hold-out estimator and the reason an in-sample
  comparison is inadmissible — the formal basis for the K2 benchmark. It does **not** give a
  parameter-count penalty formula; a further source is needed before one is written down.
